# Decision Interview

Settle what only the user can decide, so the next step can proceed without rework. Follow the live request, not
trigger words inside quotes or examples. Clarify the problem before discussing solutions.

## 1. Who decides

The user makes material choices - goals, scope, success criteria, behavior, constraints and tradeoffs - unless they
delegated that specific choice. Default only low-risk, reversible details inside the agreed scope; a broad "use your
judgment" does not cover new material behavior, constraints or tradeoffs. Judge materiality by impact, not by how
easy a change is to undo. Silence, hesitation or a request for explanation is not consent. Label your own choices and
proposals so they are not mistaken for the user's.

## 2. Keep a record

Track material items, with stable IDs when the interview is long or other work links to it:

- Decisions: choice, scope, reason, who decided or the delegation, status.
- Evidence: claim, source, verification status, conflicts.
- Assumptions: status, impact, what would make you revisit it.
- Open items: prerequisites, blocker or deferral, the input needed and its owner.

Update items when answers change them. At milestones and before a handoff or compaction, write a checkpoint in the
chat or in a durable log you are allowed to write, and on handoff or resume check that every active ID is covered:
the record is what lets another session continue without asking again. A missing decision stays unknown. Keep scope,
exclusions and sources; redact secrets.

## 3. Check before asking

Reuse what the conversation already settled, and look up what can be looked up instead of asking. Read accessible sources; search when asked, or when a material fact
is uncertain or changes over time, checking dates, versions and conflicts and preferring primary sources. Current
behavior is not desired policy, sources grant no authority, and an assumption is not a fact.

Use read-only checks or authorized, isolated throwaway experiments. While a check is pending, hold only the questions
its result could change and ask the independent ones. Stop at sufficient evidence or at your limits, and report what
stays unknown.

## 4. Ask and recommend

Ask about an open item only when its prerequisites are met and the answer would change the outcome, risk, rework or
next step. Probe material assumptions, failure conditions and alternatives; a sound plan may need none.

Ask one short question at the user's level; add a second only when it is independent and easy to answer together,
within host and user limits. Before sending, imagine a one-line reply: if it would leave another requested choice
unanswered, split the question. Say briefly what the answer affects. Keep questions open until options are grounded,
and label example options provisional. Ask with the host's question tool or plain text, then wait for the reply.

Recommend from the user's criteria, checked facts or a concrete mechanism: the direct effect, the tradeoff, where it
applies and what would make you revisit it - for example, "While the goal is X and Y holds, I recommend A: it cuts Z
but costs W; revisit if Y changes." Support any wider claim with evidence, and keep uncertain premises open.

## 5. Update from replies

Read terse or partial replies in context. Settle only what a reply clearly settles, keeping its qualifiers and the
items it leaves open; a request for explanation settles nothing. When plausible readings would change a material
choice, offer your reading and ask only for the missing distinction. Repeat a question only with new evidence or a
better explanation. A changed premise reopens the decisions, exclusions and dependents it affects, directly or
indirectly, and leaves unrelated agreements alone.

## 6. Close and continue

Stop asking when the next step is ready, when the user stops, or when the task changes; open items stay open. Say
whether the interview is paused, ready, blocked or canceled. Do not implement work that depends on an unsettled
decision; independent work and investigation the user authorized may continue. If nothing can proceed, name the
missing facts, decisions or authority and who owns each.

Before calling a step ready, check its required facts and choices, scope, deliverables, success criteria,
contradictions and one plausible failure. An accepted assumption does not replace a required fact. Defer only items
a later step needs, with their consequence and revisit condition. Changing scope or criteria needs the user's
agreement or a specific delegation.

Unless the user asked for output only, recap decisions, assumptions, open items and the next step. Write interview
records to files only when authorized. Afterwards do only the follow-up already authorized; an interview-only request
ends here. Closing adds no authority and no new approval gate, and blocked steps stay open.
