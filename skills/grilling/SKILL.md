# Grilling

Test important assumptions through adaptive questions. Reuse the user's goal, scope, answers and delegation. Follow live intent, not quoted triggers; a clear task change ends the interview while relevant decisions and open items remain.

## Keep the decision tree

Map decisions and required facts and assumptions as a **design tree**. Keep a small decision record: settled items with their source, proposals, and important open items with their dependencies. Mark explicit deferrals and unavailable prerequisites. Derive questions and closure from this record; its format is optional.

Important choices belong to the user unless validly delegated. Broad delegation can cover low-risk, reversible unlisted details within accepted constraints. Distinguish realizing that result from adding material behavior, restrictions or tradeoffs requiring a user decision. Easy editing alone does not settle this. Make validly delegated choices without asking again.

Check accessible facts through permitted reads or non-mutating checks; ask only what the user must supply or decide. Hold questions whose need, meaning, options or recommendation could change with a pending or available fact check; independent ready questions may proceed. Unknowns remain unknown; current code is not desired policy, and adopting an assumption does not verify it.

## Work one round

The **frontier** holds important unanswered questions with settled prerequisites. Probe assumptions, failure conditions and alternatives that could change the outcome. A sound plan may need no challenge. Prioritize loss, rework and what answers unblock, then burden.

Choose one fact or choice that advances the frontier. Ask one short question by default; add a second only if independent and easy to answer together. Respect host and user limits. Values that vary independently and independently affect later reasoning are separate answer units; alternatives for one choice are one unit.

Draft one question about intended behavior at the user's demonstrated level. Internally try a short reply that settles one choice: if another requested decision remains unanswered, split the question and ask only the first ready choice. Examples explain without requesting extra answers. Keep unselected ready units on the frontier; dependent follow-ups wait for their prerequisite answer or fact. Prefer open questions until options are grounded. Use an available question tool or numbered text within host rules, then wait and adapt.

## Recommend and update

Recommend from user criteria, checked facts or a concrete mechanism. Explain the choice's direct change, why it serves the aim, and the material tradeoff. A starting value need not be optimal; give its bounded role and revisit condition.

Check reasons against open premises in the record. Wider benefits need other mechanisms or facts: state those conditions with the benefit, or keep to the direct effect. Apply this to options too. A recommendation's hypothetical conditions stay open until established or validly adopted.

Read terse, misspelled or incomplete replies in context; infer clear intent without demanding polished answers. Briefly reflect consequential interpretations and proceed without reconfirmation when meaning is clear. If plausible meanings change a material choice, offer a grounded interpretation or recommendation and ask only for the missing distinction; it stays open. Update only what the reply or valid delegation settles, retaining qualifiers and unanswered items. Clarification requests leave the decision open: explain without unrelated questions. Changed premises or corrected dependencies reopen affected direct and indirect conclusions, preserving unrelated agreements. Keep updates brief.

## Close from the record

Unavailable facts hold dependent steps. Continue asking otherwise-ready questions about desired policy. If no independent step remains in scope, name the missing input and its owner. Pause when the user stops; waiting for an answer is an ongoing interview.

Before closing, internally check the agreed step's coherence and consider whether one plausible failure condition could overturn it. Reopen only material gaps; otherwise close. Required decisions must be settled, required facts established, and material assumptions accepted or validly delegated. Deferrals need understood consequences or revisit conditions and cannot satisfy an essential prerequisite. No unresolved blocker or material contradiction may remain. Scope changes require agreement.

Close with decisions and reasons, assumptions, important remaining items and their status, and the agreed next step. Use the conversation unless a file was requested. Do not implement during the interview. After valid closure, perform already-authorized work; closure adds neither authority nor a standing approval gate.
